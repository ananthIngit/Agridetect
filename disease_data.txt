Disease Name: Citrus Black spot
Pathogen Type: Fungus (Phyllosticta citricarpa)
Symptoms:
- **Hard Spot:** Small, dark, slightly raised spots (pinheads) appear on ripening fruit, often surrounded by a dark red to black ring.
- **Freckle Spot:** Tiny, red, slightly sunken spots that can resemble freckles, found on fruit.
- **False Melanose:** Irregular, raised, dark spots on leaves and green fruit.
- **Leaf Drop:** Severe infection can lead to premature leaf drop.

Treatment & Management:
- **Immediate Action:** Remove and destroy infected fallen fruit and leaves, as the pathogen overwinters in debris.
- **Chemical Control:** Apply fungicides containing **Copper** (Copper Oxychloride or Copper Hydroxide) during the susceptible period (petal fall until fruit is 4 months old).
- **Pruning:** Prune heavily infested branches and dead wood to increase air flow.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Sanitation:** Rake up debris religiously throughout the year.
- **Exclusion:** Use windbreaks to prevent the spread of spores.

Disease Name: Citrus canker
Pathogen Type: Bacteria (Xanthomonas citri subsp. citri)
Symptoms:
- **Lesions:** Raised, tan to brown lesions with water-soaked margins and a distinct yellow halo appear on leaves, stems, and fruit.
- **Texture:** Lesions become rough, corky, and crater-like over time.
- **Fruit Drop:** Premature drop of fruit.

Treatment & Management:
- **Immediate Action:** This is a highly contagious, regulated disease. Infected trees must often be removed and destroyed (incinerated) based on local regulations.
- **Chemical Control:** **Copper spray** applications can suppress the disease, but cannot cure it. Apply frequently during new flush periods.
- **Pruning:** Remove and destroy all infected branches immediately, disinfecting tools (with 10% bleach) after every cut.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Windbreaks:** Use windbreaks to reduce wind damage, as wounds are primary entry points for the bacteria.
- **Resistant Varieties:** Plant tolerant or resistant citrus varieties if available.

Disease Name: Citrus greening
Pathogen Type: Bacteria (Candidatus Liberibacter asiaticus)
Symptoms:
- **Mottling:** Asymmetrical, blotchy yellow mottling (chlorosis) on leaves that does not cross the central vein cleanly. This is the hallmark symptom.
- **Vein Swelling:** Yellow veins and thickened, enlarged midribs.
- **Fruit:** Small, lopsided, poorly colored fruit that remains green on the bottom end, often with aborted seeds.
- **Taste:** Fruit juice is often bitter and salty.

Treatment & Management:
- **Immediate Action:** Control the **Asian Citrus Psyllid (ACP)**, the insect vector that transmits the disease, using systemic insecticides.
- **Removal:** Remove and destroy infected trees or branches immediately to prevent spread, as there is currently **no effective cure**.
- **Nutrients:** Provide supplemental micronutrients (Zinc, Manganese, Iron) to help mitigate symptom severity, but this does not cure the disease.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Psyllid Control:** Maintain a rigorous insecticide program to keep the ACP population low.
- **Monitoring:** Regularly scout for ACP and early leaf symptoms.

Disease Name: Corn Common rust
Pathogen Type: Fungus (Puccinia sorghi)
Symptoms:
- **Pustules:** Small, cinnamon-brown, powdery pustules (blisters) appear on both upper and lower leaf surfaces. These pustules contain spores and can be rubbed off onto fingers.
- **Color Change:** As the infection progresses, pustules turn dark brown to black.
- **Damage:** Severe infection causes leaves to yellow, die prematurely, and reduces grain filling, leading to yield loss.

Treatment & Management:
- **Fungicides:** Apply a foliar fungicide (e.g., containing **azoxystrobin** or **propiconazole**) as soon as rust is detected, particularly on high-value corn or seed corn.
- **Timing:** Fungicides are most effective if applied before the rust becomes widespread (usually during the VT-R3 growth stages).

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Resistant Hybrids:** Plant corn hybrids labeled as resistant or tolerant to common rust.
- **Planting Date:** Planting early can help the crop mature before the rust spores build up later in the season.

Disease Name: Corn Gray leaf spot
Pathogen Type: Fungus (Cercospora zeae-maydis)
Symptoms:
- **Lesions:** Long, narrow, rectangular, pale-tan to gray spots that are restricted by the leaf veins. Lesions are typically 1 to 2 inches long.
- **Location:** Spots often appear first on the lower leaves and progress upwards.
- **Damage:** Heavy spotting causes leaves to die, leading to a gray or scorched appearance of the field.

Treatment & Management:
- **Fungicides:** Apply fungicides containing **strobilurins** or **triazoles** if the disease appears early and conditions are favorable for spread.
- **Residue Management:** Plowing under infected residue can significantly reduce the amount of pathogen available for the next season.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Crop Rotation:** Rotate corn with non-host crops (like soybeans) for at least two years.
- **Resistant Hybrids:** Use resistant or tolerant corn hybrids.
- **Tillage:** Use tillage practices that bury corn residue.

Disease Name: Corn Northern Leaf Blight
Pathogen Type: Fungus (Exserohilum turcicum)
Symptoms:
- **Lesions:** Large, cigar-shaped, grayish-green to tan lesions that can grow up to 6 inches long.
- **Appearance:** Lesions appear first on the lower leaves and progress to the upper leaves, resembling cigar butts.
- **Damage:** Causes rapid leaf necrosis (death), especially during cool, moist weather, severely limiting photosynthesis.

Treatment & Management:
- **Fungicides:** Apply a tank mix of triazole and strobilurin fungicides if the disease is identified early and environmental conditions favor the disease.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Resistant Hybrids:** Plant hybrids labeled as resistant (e.g., those with *Ht* genes).
- **Residue Management:** Rotate crops with a non-host crop and till or chop residue to speed decomposition.

Disease Name: Grape Black Measles
Pathogen Type: Fungi (A complex of fungi, including Phaeoacremonium and Phaeomoniella spp.)
Symptoms:
- **"Measles" (Eutypa dieback):** On fruit, light brown spots develop into purplish-black spots, resembling measles. Fruit clusters may dry up or rot.
- **Leaf/Shoot Symptoms:** Leaves show interveinal yellowing (chlorosis) or reddish-brown spotting. Shoots often appear stunted or weak.
- **Wood Symptoms:** Internal wood symptoms include dark spotting or streaking in the trunk and cordons.

Treatment & Management:
- **Pruning:** Remove and destroy infected wood, cutting back to healthy tissue.
- **Protection:** Treat large pruning wounds immediately with a wound protectant or fungicide paste to prevent re-infection.
- **Nutrients:** Ensure balanced nutrition to help vines tolerate infection.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Avoid Pruning in Rain:** Avoid pruning vines during periods of rain or high humidity when spores are active.
- **Sanitation:** Dispose of pruned wood away from the vineyard.

Disease Name: Grape Black rot
Pathogen Type: Fungus (Guignardia bidwellii)
Symptoms:
- **Leaf Spots:** Small, circular, reddish-brown to black spots appear on the leaves.
- **Fruit:** Berries turn black and shrivel into hard, black, wrinkled mummies (shriveled raisins).
- **Shoot Cankers:** Elongated black lesions or cankers on the shoots.

Treatment & Management:
- **Fungicides:** Apply fungicides (e.g., mancozeb, myclobutanil) starting at bud break and continuing through bloom and fruit set.
- **Removal:** Remove and destroy all mummified berries hanging on the vine or lying on the ground, as they are a major source of infection.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Sanitation:** Bury or remove mummified berries and debris before the growing season starts.
- **Airflow:** Prune vines to improve air circulation and sunlight penetration.

Disease Name: Grape Isariopsis Leaf Spot
Pathogen Type: Fungus (Isariopsis clavispora)
Symptoms:
- **Leaf Spots:** Irregular, yellowish spots appear on the upper leaf surface.
- **Fruiting:** On the underside of the leaves, dark, velvety tufts of fungal growth (spores) are visible in the corresponding spots.
- **Damage:** Severe defoliation can occur late in the season, weakening the vine.

Treatment & Management:
- **Fungicides:** Use fungicides targeting leaf spot diseases (often the same ones used for Black Rot, like Mancozeb).
- **Timing:** Fungicides are generally applied early in the season to protect new growth.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Sanitation:** Remove infected leaves and debris in the autumn.
- **Airflow:** Ensure good air circulation through proper pruning and trellising.

Disease Name: Potato Early blight
Pathogen Type: Fungus (Alternaria solani)
Symptoms:
- **Leaf Spots:** Dark brown to black spots with concentric rings, giving a distinctive **bullseye** or **target-spot** appearance. These appear first on older, lower leaves.
- **Necrosis:** Leaves around the spots turn yellow and die prematurely (necrosis).
- **Tubers:** Dark, slightly sunken lesions with purple margins can appear on the tubers.

Treatment & Management:
- **Chemical Control:** Apply fungicides containing **chlorothalonil** or **mancozeb**. Start spraying when symptoms first appear and repeat on a regular schedule.
- **Hilling:** Ensure deep hilling to prevent spores from washing down and infecting tubers.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Crop Rotation:** Rotate potatoes with non-host crops for at least two years.
- **Residue:** Plow under or remove all crop residue immediately after harvest.
- **Watering:** Avoid overhead irrigation late in the day or during humid periods.

Disease Name: Potato Late blight
Pathogen Type: Oomycete/Water Mold (Phytophthora infestans)
Symptoms:
- **Lesions:** Irregularly shaped, dark green to black, water-soaked spots on leaves and stems.
- **Fuzzy Growth:** During cool, humid periods, a **white, fuzzy fungal growth** (sporangia) can be seen on the underside of the lesions.
- **Odor:** Infected fields may emit a strong, foul odor.
- **Tubers:** Irregular, reddish-brown, dry, corky rot beneath the skin.

Treatment & Management:
- **Chemical Control (Urgent):** Requires immediate and aggressive application of highly effective fungicides (e.g., those containing **mefenoxam** or **propamocarb**). Preventive sprays are highly recommended in susceptible areas.
- **Removal:** Immediately destroy infected foliage by burning or burying to prevent spore spread.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **"Seed" Control:** Plant only certified, disease-free seed potatoes.
- **Cull Pile:** Eliminate all cull piles (discarded potatoes) and volunteer potatoes, as they are primary sources of early infection.

Disease Name: Tomato Early_blight
Pathogen Type: Fungus (Alternaria solani)
Symptoms:
- **Leaf Spots:** Dark brown to black spots (1/4 inch diameter) often develop with concentric rings, giving a distinctive "bullseye" or **target-spot** appearance. They typically appear first on the older, lower leaves.
- **Defoliation:** Heavily infected leaves turn yellow and drop prematurely.
- **Stem/Fruit:** Small, dark, sunken lesions may appear on the stems or near the stem end of the fruit.

Treatment & Management:
- **Chemical Control:** Apply fungicides containing **chlorothalonil** or **copper-based products**. Start applications immediately when symptoms first appear.
- **Removal:** Immediately remove and destroy infected lower leaves and plant debris to reduce fungal spores.

Prevention & Cultural Practices:
- **Crop Rotation:** Do not plant tomatoes or potatoes in the same spot for 3 years.
- **Watering:** Water at the base of the plant using drip irrigation; avoid overhead watering.
- **Air Circulation:** Stake plants to keep foliage off the ground.
